CleanTech Open Evaluator FAQs & Guidelines

Official diligence rules, grading thresholds, and scoring standards.

📐 What are the "Large" vs "Small" Brackets in the Business Model Canvas?

The CleanTech Open rubric evaluates the 9 standard Strategyzer Business Model Canvas blocks divided into Large Brackets (evaluated in BC_Q4) and Small Brackets (evaluated in BC_Q5):

Rubric ID Classification Strategyzer Color Included Canvas Blocks
BC_Q4 Large / Big Brackets
(Core Pillars)		 🔵 Blue Blocks 1. Key Partners
2. Value Propositions
3. Customer Segments
4. Cost Structure
5. Revenue Streams
BC_Q5 Small Brackets
(Connectors)		 🟡 Yellow Blocks 1. Key Activities
2. Key Resources
3. Customer Relationships
4. Channels

Visual Rule: The 4 corner foundation blocks + center value proposition form the Large Brackets (5). The operational connecting blocks form the Small Brackets (4).

🔍 Can I Google search if information is not found in the documents?

Yes. You are explicitly authorized and encouraged to Google search the company, verify founder claims, check their live website, inspect patent databases, and research customer validations if the submitted PDFs lack specific details.

📁 What if a startup has files but no dedicated BMC document?

If no standalone Business Model Canvas PDF was submitted, use the dropdown above the PDF viewer to inspect other founder deliverables (especially their Investor Pitch Deck and Executive Summary) or web research. Score the BMC questions based on that evidence. Only award 0 PTS if no business model details exist anywhere.

🎯 Strict Scoping vs Flexible Cross-Referencing

  • Strict Scoping: Business Canvas (BC), Executive Summary (IS), and Investor Pitch (IP) must be evaluated primarily on their dedicated deliverables (EBD1, EBD6, EBD8).
  • Flexible Cross-Referencing: For all other modules (Financials, Tech Validation, Product-Market Fit, Team, Legal, Environmental Impact), you may freely cross-reference any submitted file or online source to verify founder assertions.

💬 How should technical jargon or buzzwords be scored?

In the Value Proposition, Branding, Marketing, and Pitch modules: using 1–2 technical terms to explain a scientific process is fine. However, if a founder relies on dense, unexplained buzzwords that obscure their real business model or technology, deduct points for lack of clarity.

⚖️ Do I need to calculate weights, averages, or decimals?

No. In historical spreadsheets, reviewers had to deal with manual formulas and decimal weights. The modern Diligence Engine standardizes all 282 criteria onto a clean 0 to 1 point scale (0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0 PTS). Any parenthetical numbers in the prompt (e.g. (1-5) or (0.44)) are legacy references preserved for historical record.

⚡ Deliverable Priority: Application Phase vs EBD Phase

Always prioritize the Essential Business Deliverables (EBD) Phase files. EBD represents the finalized post-program materials (e.g. validated 3-year pro-forma financial models, customer interview sheets). Only refer to early Application Phase survey text if an EBD document was not submitted.

🔄 Consistency & Calibration Review

Once you have scored all startups assigned to you, review your score distributions across the cohort to confirm that your evaluation standards were applied uniformly from your first startup to your last.
